Commercial trucking is governed by strict federal Hours of Service (HOS) regulations. Since 2017, the majority of carriers have been required to use Electronic Logging Devices (ELD) to record driving time automatically. However, the pressure of just-in-time logistics often leads to dangerous tampering.

## The Myth of the "Uneditable" Log

Many victims assume that because the logs are digital, they are infallible. In reality, "ghost logs," "double-logging," and intentional sensor disconnection are rampant. 

## Metadata: The Smoking Gun

When we seize the ELD data, we don't just look at the hours reported. We audit the metadata:
- **GPS Inconsistencies:** Does the truck's reported location match toll records from the NJ Turnpike?
- **Engine Data Pings:** Was the engine running during a reported "rest" period?
- **User Edits:** We track every manual override made by dispatchers in the central office.

Securing this data within the first 48 hours is critical, as data overwrite protocols can erase evidence if spoliation letters are not filed immediately.
